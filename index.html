About the State

A civilization on the Ganges plain

Bihar sits on the fertile plains of the Ganga in eastern India, bordered by Nepal to the north. Its capital, Patna, was once Pataliputra — capital of the Magadha, Mauryan and Gupta empires, among the greatest cities of the ancient world. For over two and a half thousand years this land has produced empires, universities, religions and revolutions.

Iconic History

Two and a half millennia in six chapters

From the world's earliest republics to India's freedom struggle, Bihar's timeline reads like the spine of Indian civilization itself.

c. 599 BCE

Mahavira & the Vajji Confederacy

Near Vaishali, Mahavira — the 24th Jain Tirthankara — was born. The surrounding Vajji confederacy, led by the Licchavis, is widely cited by historians as one of the earliest known republics in recorded history, governed by an assembly rather than a monarch.

c. 528 BCE

Enlightenment at Bodh Gaya

Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment under a Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya, becoming the Buddha. The site remains one of the four most sacred places in Buddhism and draws pilgrims from across the world.

c. 320–185 BCE

Magadha & the Mauryan Empire

Ruling from Pataliputra, Chandragupta Maurya and later his grandson Ashoka the Great built one of the largest empires in ancient India. Ashoka's edicts, inscribed on pillars and rocks across the subcontinent, still stand as some of the earliest deciphered writings in Indian history.

5th–12th century CE

Nalanda — the world's oldest residential university

Nalanda Mahavihara drew scholars from China, Korea, Tibet and Central Asia to study philosophy, medicine, mathematics and logic. Today its ruins are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and a modern Nalanda University has since been re-established nearby.

1666 CE

Birth of Guru Gobind Singh

Patna Sahib marks the birthplace of Guru Gobind Singh, the tenth Sikh Guru, making the city one of Sikhism's most revered pilgrimage sites.

1917 CE

The Champaran Satyagraha

In Champaran, Mahatma Gandhi led his first act of civil disobedience in India, championing indigo farmers against exploitative British planters — a movement that set the template for the freedom struggle to come.

Heritage

Famous Places of Bihar

From a Bodhi tree that changed the world to the ruins of the planet's oldest university, Bihar's landmarks are not museum pieces — they are still living sites of pilgrimage, study and memory.

Gaya District
Mahabodhi Temple, Bodh Gaya

Built beside the descendant of the very tree under which the Buddha attained enlightenment, this towering temple is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Buddhism's four holiest places.

Nalanda District
Nalanda Mahavihara

The red-brick ruins of the world's oldest residential university, once home to thousands of monks and scholars from across Asia. A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2016.

Rajgir
Vishwa Shanti Stupa, Rajgir

A gleaming white peace pagoda crowning Ratnagiri hill, reached by ropeway, overlooking the ancient city where Buddha and Mahavira both once taught.

Vaishali
Vaishali

Seat of the ancient Vajji republic and site of the Buddha's last sermon, marked today by a polished Ashokan pillar topped with a single lion, standing sentinel for over two millennia.

Patna
Takht Sri Patna Sahib

One of Sikhism's five Takhts, built at the birthplace of Guru Gobind Singh — a domed gurdwara on the Ganga's bank that draws pilgrims from around the world.

Patna
Golghar

A colossal beehive-shaped granary built by the British in 1786 after a devastating famine — climb its spiral outer staircase for one of the finest views of the Ganga.

Culture & Craft

Art that is still practiced, not preserved

Madhubani Painting

Originating in the Mithila region, this folk art uses natural dyes and geometric patterns to depict deities, nature and festivals — traditionally painted on freshly plastered mud walls.

Sujani Embroidery

A running-stitch quilting craft traditionally made from layers of old cloth, telling stories through embroidered scenes of village life.

Bidesia & Folk Music

A theatrical folk tradition founded by Bhikhari Thakur, blending song, dance and drama around themes of migration and separation.

Terracotta & Clay Craft

Villages across Bihar continue centuries-old traditions of terracotta pottery and figurines, often shaped for ritual and festival use.

Cuisine

Flavors built for the field and the festival

Litti Chokha

Roasted wheat balls stuffed with spiced sattu, served with a smoky mash of roasted eggplant, tomato and potato.

Sattu

Roasted gram flour, mixed into drinks or parathas — a staple prized for keeping the body cool through Bihar's summers.

Thekua

A dense, sweet wheat cookie flavored with jaggery and fennel, made especially during Chhath Puja.

Khaja

Flaky, syrup-soaked layered pastry from Silao, near Nalanda — a sweet with a centuries-old reputation.

Language & Literature

Four tongues, one land

Alongside Hindi, Bihar is home to a rich family of regional languages — each with its own literature, poetry and oral tradition, including the 14th-century Maithili poet Vidyapati, whose verses influenced poetry across eastern India.
